Six forecasts were locked in July. Reality answered on 2 August 2026.

An open forecasting engine predicted what seven OPEC+ countries would decide about September oil production. The predictions were sealed by cryptographic hash before the meeting, and scored afterwards by an arithmetic rule written in advance. This is what happened.

The reading

What it actually shows

The research mattered more than the method.

The same question was asked twice: once when the engine knew little beyond last month's decision, and once after it had found each country's budget pressures, repayment obligations for past over-production, and quota record.

Every method improved. The two mathematical models moved a point or so; the AI judgement moved 8 points. That difference is itself informative — the models are anchored to the group's last decision, while unanchored judgement swings with whatever evidence it is given.

And what it does not show.

  • One graded question is not a track record. The system refuses to rank its own methods until ten have been graded.
  • Analysts widely expected this outcome, so the question had little power to separate good forecasters from lucky ones.
  • The perfect score is exact only after rounding — the raw value was 65.67 against a forecast of 66.0.
  • A second scoring method, which judges the whole predicted range rather than its midpoint, ranks the two mathematical models the other way round. Both are reported.

Every forecast above carries a SHA-256 published before the event and anchored in the Bitcoin blockchain. Anyone can recompute them.
