## commentary-body
The same question was asked twice: once when the engine knew little beyond last month's decision,
and once after it had found each country's budget pressures, repayment obligations for past
over-production, and quota record.

Every method improved. The two mathematical models moved a point or so; the AI judgement moved
{{llm_shift}} points. That difference is itself informative — the models are anchored to the
group's last decision, while unanchored judgement swings with whatever evidence it is given.

## caveats
- One graded question is not a track record. The system refuses to rank its own methods until ten
  have been graded.
- Analysts widely expected this outcome, so the question had little power to separate good
  forecasters from lucky ones.
- The perfect score is exact only after rounding — the raw value was {{actual_raw_continuum}}
  against a forecast of {{best_median}}.
- A second scoring method, which judges the whole predicted range rather than its midpoint, ranks
  the two mathematical models the other way round. Both are reported.
