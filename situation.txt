QUESTION
========
question_id: Q-2026-USIRAN-STAGE2

primary question (binary): Will the United States and Iran advance from the
first stage of the 17 June 2026 memorandum of understanding into the wider
"stage two" negotiations envisaged by that MOU, on or before 31 August 2026?
(The MOU's initial period is 60 days from 17 June, ending around 16 August.)

BACKGROUND (all facts paraphrased from the sources listed in sources/;
primary anchor: UK House of Commons Library briefing CBP-10637, updated
July 2026)
==========================================================================

The war. On 28 February 2026, Israel and the United States began a series of
air strikes on Iran, stating the aims of regime change and destroying Iran's
nuclear and ballistic-missile programmes. Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was
killed in the strikes; Iran appointed his son, Mojtaba Khamenei, as
successor. Iran retaliated against US bases, Israel, and sites in Arab Gulf
states, and closed the Strait of Hormuz. Hezbollah attacked Israel from
Lebanon and Israel launched ground operations there. [Commons CBP-10637]

The ceasefire. After roughly six weeks of conflict, Pakistan's Prime
Minister announced on 8 April that the US and Iran had agreed a conditional
two-week ceasefire, with Pakistan mediating talks. The ceasefire was later
extended, with the US President saying it would hold until discussions
conclude "one way or another." First direct talks in Islamabad on 11 April
made progress but produced no agreement: the US side said most points were
agreed except the nuclear one; Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi said a
deal was very close but blamed maximalist US demands. [Commons CBP-10637]

The MOU. On 17 June 2026, the US and Iran signed a 14-point memorandum of
understanding. It sets out a three-stage process: an initial 60-day period of
talks covering freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's nuclear
and missile programmes, and sanctions. Stage one requires five measures that
the parties must begin implementing before wider (stage two) talks can occur.
The text states that Iran will not have a nuclear weapon. [Commons CBP-10637,
June-July 2026 update]

The core dispute — enrichment. The standing US position is zero uranium
enrichment in Iran; Iran insists on retaining the right to enrich while
saying the amount and level are negotiable. Reported bridging ideas include a
time-limited enrichment moratorium (a US proposal of around 20 years against
an Iranian counter of about 5) and transferring enriched uranium abroad
(offered by Russia), which Iran's foreign ministry has flatly refused.
As of June 2025 the IAEA had verified an Iranian stockpile of more than
400 kg enriched to 60%. [Commons CBP-10637]

Verification. IAEA inspectors have had no access to Iranian nuclear sites
since the June 2025 strikes, so the agency cannot verify the programme's
current state or damage. IAEA chief Rafael Grossi has said an agreement
without inspections would be illusory; by June 2026 he signalled inspections
would happen under the interim arrangement while calling their timing not
essential. The US and Iran have made contradictory statements about whether
and when sites will be inspected. [Commons CBP-10637; CBS News, June 2026]

Other issues on the table. (1) Hormuz: both sides maintain blockades (Iran's
closure; a US counter-blockade of ships bound for Iranian ports); the US,
UK, and UAE demand unconditional reopening, while Iran has floated a new
security arrangement for the waterway and toll charges. (2) Missiles and
proxies: the reported US 15-point plan includes missile-production limits and
an end to Iranian funding of armed groups such as Hezbollah; Iran has
historically refused to negotiate its missiles, and both Hamas and Hezbollah
have rejected disarmament. (3) Reconstruction: Iran claims about USD 270
billion in war damages, demands reparations (including from Gulf states) and
release of frozen assets; President Pezeshkian has called reparations the
only way to end the conflict; the US has not engaged the demand. (4) A
long-term settlement: Iran seeks firm international guarantees against future
attack, naming possible guarantor powers including China, Pakistan, Turkey
and Russia, and rejects further temporary ceasefires. [Commons CBP-10637]

Other actors. Israel says its war aims stand and will be completed by
diplomacy or by force; its foreign minister called removal of enriched
material from Iran a precondition for ending the war. The Gulf states were
all struck by Iran and are split: the UAE (hardest hit) is most hawkish —
demanding reparations and exploring freezes of Iranian assets — while Oman
keeps a middle road and welcomed the new Supreme Leader, and Qatar stresses
coexistence. The E3 (UK, France, Germany) triggered UN snapback sanctions in
October 2025 and, with the EU, back a comprehensive deal with real nuclear
expertise at the table. China and Pakistan issued a joint five-point plan
(sovereignty, reopening the strait); Russia welcomes talks, opposes
sanctions, and has offered to take Iran's enriched uranium. The US has
deployed additional forces to the region and says they stay until a real
agreement is complied with. [Commons CBP-10637]
